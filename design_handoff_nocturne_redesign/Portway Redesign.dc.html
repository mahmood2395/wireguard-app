Turn 2 · Last handshake, five readings

The datum is one age, so give it a limit

A handshake age on its own is unreadable — twelve seconds and ninety seconds both mean "fine", and only the 180-second cutoff, where WireGuard treats the peer as gone, makes the number mean anything. Each of these draws the age against that limit rather than as a bare figure. They sit at home-card width so you can drop one straight into the hero.

2a is the one that shipped — it now sits on the home hero, the 1d home screen and the config detail below, replacing the heartbeat lane. The other four stay here as a record. All five are live off the same clock; connect the phone in 1d and they run.

Turn 1 · Portway on Nocturne

A VPN for someone who was handed a config

The clay material is gone. It was a beautiful piece of engineering — two blurred shadows, a software layer, a generated nav puck — and it cost the screens 21dp of gutter on every side of every surface, which is why the current Connect screen reads as five objects floating in a field rather than one product. Nocturne's answer to depth on a dark ground is an edge plus ambient darkness: a 1px hairline, an 8px radius, and the accent spent as a line or a glow rather than a fill. Every surface below is that.

The second change is vocabulary. Portway has no server list — what a user owns is a set of configs someone handed them. So "Tunnels" becomes Configs, the public key moves behind a disclosure, and the city shown in the ring is resolved from the peer's IP rather than chosen from a menu — a fact about where your traffic actually surfaces, not a product promise.

The third change is the handshake. "12 seconds ago" re-renders every second and says nothing — a number that never sits still is a number nobody reads. It becomes a decay bar (2a): the whole scale is WireGuard's 180-second cutoff, where the peer counts as gone, with a hairline at the point the rekey is due. The age still ticks, but it now has a limit to tick against — one glance says how much room is left, and a bar past the hairline is the news.

One deliberate loss worth flagging: JetBrains Mono for figures is dropped. Nocturne is Inter over Inter, so figures here are Inter with tabular numerals. It ticks in place just as well and saves the 1.1 MB of bundled fonts — but the mono had character, and if you want it back it is one token.

The most Nocturne-faithful reading: the accent is a rule that draws itself across the screen as the handshake lands. State is carried by type size and one line. Tap the status block.
