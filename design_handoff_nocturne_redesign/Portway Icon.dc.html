Turn 2 · Launcher icon

Four marks, one fill each

Your existing icon comment already states the hard constraint, and it is the right one: one path, one fill, so the Android 13 monochrome variant survives tinting instead of collapsing into a blob. Every mark below obeys it — negative space is done with fillType="evenOdd", never with a second colour, and each glyph is drawn on currentColor. All four sit inside the dashed 66dp safe circle of the 108dp adaptive canvas, so nothing clips when a launcher masks to a circle, a squircle or a teardrop.

Ground is #262a60 — Nocturne's one sanctioned saturated field, the same value the deck uses for section dividers. An icon is exactly that case: presence at small scale. The glyph is #d2cefd (accent-300), which is a 9:1 pair. The 24px column is the one that matters — that is the notification and Quick Settings tile size, and it is where three of these four survive and one does not.

2aKeyhole shield, corrected

Your current mark, scaled to 0.82 so it actually clears the safe circle — today it pokes 4dp past it and a circular launcher shaves the shield's shoulders. Nothing else changes. The safest option: no brand relearning, and it still reads as security rather than as a network utility.

Against it: a shield with a keyhole is the single most crowded idea in this category, and the keyhole's 2dp stem is the first thing to disappear at 24px — look at the small column.

2bThe portway

An archway with a threshold bar across it — the name drawn literally. It is the only one of the four that says what the app does rather than how it feels about it, and it matches the onboarding line: Portway is a door, not a service.

Frame is 8dp thick and the arch apex 10dp, so nothing in it is thinner than the stroke carrying the shape — it holds at 24px. Note the sill is flush with the frame's feet rather than floating: an earlier version had it crossing the opening mid-height, which made the whole mark read as a capital A. My pick.

2cBeats

Three flush-left bars, decaying — the heartbeat lane compressed into a mark, and a direct descendant of the current Tunnels tab glyph. Nocturne's asymmetry read at icon scale: weight on the left, air on the right.

Strongest at 24px of the four, and it ties the icon to the one genuinely novel thing in the app. Against it: bars are a crowded shape too — at a glance it can read as a list or a hamburger, which is a real risk on a launcher.

2dP, bored through

A monogram whose bowl is an aperture. The most ownable of the four — a letterform is the one shape no competitor can also arrive at — and it carries the keyhole idea from 2a without drawing a shield.

Against it: the stem and bowl are separate subpaths that only touch, so any future scaling has to keep them kissing exactly or a seam opens; and the aperture closes up at 24px, where it becomes a solid P.

Grounds, on 2b

Nocturne forbids flooding the accent, and an icon is a flood by definition — so the ground is the section field, not the accent. The middle option inverts to the app's own ground, which is quieter on a dark wallpaper but nearly vanishes on a dark one too. The third is the accent at full chroma, shown so you can see why the system rules it out.

Shipping it

Three files change, and one gets added. Paste the chosen pathData below into ic_launcher_foreground.xml, set ic_launcher_background to the ground, and delete the mipmap-* PNGs — an adaptive icon needs no raster buckets. Add a <monochrome> entry to mipmap-anydpi-v26/ic_launcher.xml pointing at the same foreground drawable, which is what the one-fill rule buys you. Reuse the same path for ic_portway_mark.xml (settings row) and ic_tile.xml (Quick Settings), and redraw tv_logo_banner.xml from it.
